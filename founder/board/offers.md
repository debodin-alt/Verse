# Offers lens
Score: 5/10 | Vote: FUND IF

## What is strong

This applies the published Offers framework summarised in the installed founder-board lens; it is not an endorsement from its author. The score is a qualitative assessment of the current business evidence. FUND IF supports a bounded validation and productionisation effort, conditional on Daniel's budget and scope decisions; it does not establish demand, profitability or a release specification.

The founder has a concrete problem: songwriting ideas get mixed with unrelated notes, while opening a production tool feels too involved for brainstorming. A proposed offer hypothesis is **“Capture a song idea quickly, then return to develop it in one dedicated notebook.”** Its value comes from preserving creative momentum and making unfinished work accessible. Price and purchase model remain explicitly undecided.

Market assessment, using qualitative evidence ratings:

- **Pain: demonstrated for the founder; intensity across buyers unknown.** Daniel already describes the capture-to-development problem, but this is one person's experience.
- **Money to spend: unknown.** An intention to sell a full app supplies no evidence about a buyer's budget or willingness to pay.
- **Ease of reaching buyers: unknown.** No initial segment or acquisition route has been validated.
- **Market growth: unknown.** There is no market research supporting a growth claim. The market-selection test therefore remains open.

Value-equation assessment, also qualitative:

- **Dream outcome: promising but unvalidated beyond Daniel.** Keeping words, melody and backing connected could help a songwriter return to an idea. Finishing better songs is a larger outcome the present evidence cannot promise.
- **Perceived likelihood: weak evidence.** Existing catalogue, text capture and audio features make a demonstration possible. Browser checks and mocked tests do not prove dependable recording or preservation of irreplaceable work on real devices.
- **Time to result: promising, unmeasured.** Immediate text capture could yield an early useful result; capture speed and later retrieval have not been measured with prospective buyers.
- **Effort and sacrifice: mixed.** The dedicated dashboard matches Daniel's preference, but manual backup, unproven device transitions and headphone behaviour may add effort. These need observation, not assumptions about new accounts or sync services.

A lean offer stack can start from existing capabilities: capture a lyric or fragment; find it through the catalogue and tags; develop it with backing and takes; retrieve or export the work. Each addresses a step towards the same outcome. Reliability and understandable recovery may add more value than extra bonuses. Separate voice/backing and an exported mix are desired capabilities whose implementation scope is still open. Do not advertise them as established benefits.

## What would kill it (the top 3, most dangerous first)

1. **The problem does not support a reachable paying segment.** Founder satisfaction could coexist with buyers who rarely feel the pain or will not pay to solve it. Market size, willingness to pay and acquisition remain unknown.
2. **The promised convenience fails at the moment an idea matters.** Lost work, failed recording, confusing exports or unsuitable wireless-headphone routing would reduce confidence in the offer. These are unresolved risks, not observed failures. A guarantee cannot substitute for tested preservation and recovery.
3. **Delivering the offer costs more effort and money than buyers will support.** Multiple platforms, audio support and customer operations could make a seemingly simple notebook expensive to sustain. No operating budget, support allowance or financial model exists, so no margin or pricing conclusion is justified.

## The questions I would ask the founder

- Which specific songwriter should recognise this problem immediately, and where can you reach people with that workflow?
- What recent incident shows the pain is consequential enough for them to switch tools and pay? What do they use today?
- What must happen in a first session for them to believe Verse works, and what makes them return to an unfinished idea?
- Which existing experience must remain familiar, and what backup, export and device expectations must the offer explicitly address?
- What purchase-model preference, operating budget and maintenance commitment should constrain the next commercial hypothesis? These are pending interview decisions.
- What evidence would make a higher price still feel justified through saved effort and trusted results? No doubling-price claim is supported today.

## If FUND IF: the conditions

- Validate a narrow buyer and the capture-to-development problem with real prospective customers. Test demonstrated behaviour, return use and willingness to pay separately. `/founder-consumer` may help structure hypotheses; simulated buyers cannot establish demand.
- Research buyers' actual alternatives through `/founder-competitors`; make no superiority or cheaper-price claim before that comparison.
- Demonstrate the proposed promise on the agreed initial devices and workflows, including real recording, headphones, preservation, recovery and export. Resolve rollout and technical scope with Daniel before committing to an offer.
- Agree an explicit price/model hypothesis and operating limits after the pending interview; use `/founder-cfo` to assess costs and sustainability once inputs exist. A payment-free v1 remains acceptable and does not itself test paid demand.
- Keep any promise or guarantee narrow, measurable and supportable. No creative-success guarantee, invented scarcity or urgency is justified. If an early offer needs bonuses to make its core outcome attractive, revisit the core promise first.
