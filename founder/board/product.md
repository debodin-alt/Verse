# Product lens
Score: 7/10 | Vote: FUND IF

## What is strong

- **A clear product sentence:** Verse helps songwriters capture a lyric or melody quickly, then return to develop it in one dedicated notebook. This is a proposed focus grounded in Daniel's workflow, not a validated market claim.
- **The experience has a useful centre.** Daniel already likes the catalogue/dashboard and most of the existing app. Text, fragments, songs, poems and takes offer a foundation for continuity from an unfinished thought to a developed idea. Preserve that familiarity and test changes against it.
- **Work back from the creative moment.** A proposed first-five-minute experience is: open Verse when a line arrives, save it without setup, find it in the catalogue, reopen it and continue. A melody or backing-first session is another entry into the same saved creative work. The promise is coherent even though reliable recording and cross-device behaviour remain unproven.
- **There is room for disciplined craft.** Existing backup and export capabilities are relevant foundations. Their presence does not establish recoverability; verifying the less visible details can make the visible simplicity trustworthy.

This is a qualitative, conditional product/business assessment using the supplied published-framework summary. It is not an endorsement by its source, demand validation or an investment return estimate. Price and purchase model remain undecided.

## What would kill it (the top 3, most dangerous first)

1. **A notebook that cannot be trusted with an irreplaceable idea.** Local storage, updates, restore, recording interruptions and device transitions are seams where work could be lost or appear missing. Refresh persistence for one text capture is insufficient evidence. A damaged catalogue or failed recovery would undermine the entire promise.
2. **Recording that breaks at the moment of inspiration.** Real iPhone microphone behaviour, wireless headphone routing, latency, interruptions and mix quality are unverified. Existing mixed takes do not satisfy the requested separate voice/backing capability. If capturing a tune requires troubleshooting, or a saved/exported result differs materially from what the creator expected, the product fails its experience test.
3. **Breadth that destroys the simple reason to open Verse.** Serving three platforms while expanding editing, sync, billing and audio could bury quick capture and destabilise an experience Daniel already likes. Conversely, pleasing the founder alone would not establish that other songwriters understand, return to or eventually pay for the product. Both scope discipline and external observation are necessary.

## The questions I would ask the founder

- What single successful creative session should make someone choose Verse again: capturing a line, developing an old fragment, or freestyling over backing? Which must be excellent first?
- What would you cut or defer to protect that session while preserving the current catalogue? How much separation and editing of voice/backing is actually necessary?
- What must happen when there is no connection, the phone locks, a call arrives, headphones disconnect, or the app closes unexpectedly?
- Does “iPhone and Mac” require the same catalogue on both immediately? What backup and recovery behaviour would make you comfortable entrusting your only copy to Verse?
- Which songwriters beyond Daniel share this workflow, and can we observe them capturing and later developing a real idea? Willingness to pay and the commercial model need separate research; neither is established here.
- When commercial distribution begins, who owns the experience across discovery, installation, permissions, any eventual purchase, support and recovery?

## If FUND IF: the conditions

- Agree a narrow release journey and platform order with Daniel before major implementation. Keep quick capture and return-to-development central; proposed deferrals are decisions for him, not changes to the release specification in this memo.
- Demonstrate preservation of existing data through updates, backup/restore and recovery scenarios without clearing user storage. Make device-local storage and any limits on access elsewhere understandable before users rely on them.
- Test the agreed recording, playback and export journey on Daniel's real iPhone and wireless headphones, including interruption and failure paths. Define the required voice/backing separation and acceptable saved mix before choosing architecture or promising support.
- Observe prospective users completing the core journey and returning to their own work. Use their difficulties to refine the experience; treat adoption and payment interest as open evidence questions, not assumed consequences of founder satisfaction.
- Review the whole proposed customer journey and maintenance burden before commercial launch. Resolve price/model, operating budget and support expectations separately; do not silently introduce accounts, paid services, analytics, external AI or publishing to satisfy these conditions.
